## Summary
- Names. "Astra" is OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra (API id gpt-6-astra, announced 2026-09-03, $10 input and $50 output per million tokens, 1,050,000-token context). The owner's attribution to OpenAI is correct. "Kimi" is Moonshot AI's Kimi line; the current open-weight flagship is Kimi K3.1 Pro (2.8T total and 104B active parameters, 1,048,576-token context, custom "kimi-k3.1-pro" license, weights published on Hugging Face in the first week of November 2026); Kimi K2.6 (1T, modified MIT, 2026-04-20) is the last MIT-style release. "DeepSeek" is DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 (served as deepseek-flash, 2026-10-17) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813; the V4 weights are MIT. "Opus" is Claude Opus 5.5 ($4 and $20, 1M context) and "Fable" is Claude Fable 5.1 ($10 and $50, 1M context, 2026-09-01). The other current open-weight families are Qwen3.8 (27B dense Apache-2.0; 2.4T-A95B custom license), GLM-5.3 and GLM-5.3-Flash (320B-A18B, MIT), MiniMax-M3.1-Flash (custom), Mistral Large 4 (1T-A52B, custom), Llama 4 (Meta community license, April 2025, no newer Llama found), Gemma 4 (Apache-2.0, up to 31B dense) and gpt-oss-120b and 20b (Apache-2.0). Findings 1 to 27.
- On-device versus hosted. Every closed model (Astra, Opus, Fable, Gemini) is hosted only, so each step's screenshot and extracted text leave the machine, and for workflow identification the whole capture window would leave. Of the open families, only the small members fit a typical laptop: gpt-oss-20b (16 GB), Qwen3.8-27B at 4-bit (about 16 GB), Gemma 4 (E2B to 31B), Mistral Small 4, and Ministral 3. gpt-oss-120b (59 GiB in MXFP4) needs a 96 GB or larger Apple Silicon machine. Kimi K3.1 Pro (2.8T), Kimi K2.6 (1T), DeepSeek V4 Pro (1.6T) and Flash (284B), GLM-5.3-Flash (320B), Qwen3.8 Max (2.4T), MiniMax M3 and Mistral Large 4 (1T) do not fit any laptop; they are self-host (multi-GPU server) or hosted-call options. Measured laptop throughput: a 7B Q4_0 model generates 83 tokens per second on an M4 Max (546 GB/s, 40 GPU cores) and 24 tokens per second on a base M4 under llama.cpp's community llama-bench table; Qwen3-8B 4-bit decodes at 94 tokens per second on an M4 Max GPU (apple-silicon-llm-bench, 2026-06); gpt-oss-20b reaches 115 tokens per second on an M2 Ultra desktop. No primary Windows-GPU throughput table was found (unknown). For the capture-stream job, prefill dominates: at the measured 7B prefill of about 900 tokens per second on M4 Max, a 27B model would prefill at roughly a quarter of that (estimate, not measured), so a multi-million-token capture window must be processed incrementally in the background. Findings 18 to 27.
- Retention terms. Every major vendor's default keeps API prompts and outputs for a period: OpenAI up to 30 days of abuse-monitoring logs; Anthropic deletes within 30 days, and Claude Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Fable 5 and Mythos 5 are "Covered Models" that require 30-day retention with no ZDR "unless expressly authorized" (effective 2026-06-09); Google paid tier logs "for a limited period" and does not train on it; Moonshot's Open Platform retains input "while your account is active" in Singapore and may use content to improve services unless an enterprise agreement says otherwise; DeepSeek's policy page could not be reached from this machine (DNS failure for cdn.deepseek.com) and no primary retention term is cited for it. True zero data retention exists at OpenAI (prior approval and sales contact, no public tier or spend floor, excludes Files, Batches, Assistants and similar endpoints), Anthropic (sales contact, per organization, excludes Covered Models), Google Cloud (project-level approval for OpenAI models; for Google models, ZDR means turning off each logging feature), AWS Bedrock (retention mode "none" is self-service; OpenAI models keep classifier-flagged traffic 30 days unless ZDR is granted; Claude Fable 5 and later require retention), Azure (not used for training; abuse-monitoring storage is off only after an approved "modified abuse monitoring" application for managed customers), Fireworks (ZDR by default for open models), OpenRouter (account, model-group and per-request ZDR routing, with CSAM screening in flight from 2026-10-27), Together (a settings toggle to not retain prompts or responses) and Groq (no fixed API retention stated). Whether a new consumer app can obtain OpenAI or Anthropic ZDR is not stated on any policy page; both say "subject to approval". Findings 3, 5 to 11, 28 to 36.
- Cost per task. Under the stated assumptions (one 1280x800 screenshot and 1,500 fresh text tokens per step, 600 output tokens per step, full history resent each step, list prices on 2026-11-09), a 15-step bill payment costs about $0.03 cached or $0.11 uncached on deepseek-flash, $0.23 or $0.74 on Kimi K3.1 Pro, $0.41 or $1.54 on Claude Opus 5.5, $0.95 or $3.85 on Claude Fable 5.1 and $1.14 or $3.69 on GPT-6 Astra. The full table for all seven seed categories is in evidence/researcher-5/cost-model.txt, produced by cost-model.py. A $20 monthly budget covers roughly 20 frontier bill-payment tasks cached, or about 80 on Opus 5.5, or hundreds on the open models. Findings 12, 13, 37, 38.
- Quality evidence and the owner's hypothesis. On the easier OSWorld-Verified, vendors self-report open-weight parity or better: Kimi K2.6 73.1 versus Claude Opus 4.6 72.7 (Moonshot's run), Qwen3.8-27B 84.3 versus Opus 4.6 Max 72.7 (Qwen's run). On the harder OSWorld 2.0 and V2.1, Moonshot's mixed-source table puts Kimi K3.1 Pro at 79.6 versus Claude Opus 5.5 81.8, Fable 5.1 80.7 and GPT-6 Astra 75.8, and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 69.3; Terminal-Bench 4.0 shows K3.1 Pro 65.7 versus Opus 5.5 66.4 and Fable 5.1 57.9, but DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 42.3 and GLM-5.3 41.8. Artificial Analysis (independent) ranked Kimi K2.6 fourth on its Intelligence Index (54 versus 57 for the top closed models) on 2026-04-20 and now lists Kimi K3.1 Pro as the top open-weight model. Verdict: the evidence supports the hypothesis for the largest open-weight models on general desktop and browser tasks, contradicts it for the small models that fit a laptop (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, MiniMax M3.1 Flash, 27B-class) on long-horizon computer use and terminal work, and leaves it untested for the landlord tasks themselves: no public benchmark covers utility-portal logins with 2FA, lease editing in a word processor, or payments (tau-bench's domains are airline, retail and telecom). Non-frontier models fall short most on agentic science and coding (Terminal-Bench-Science: K3.1 Pro 38.1 versus Astra 68.1; ProgramBench 31.5 versus Opus 5.5 65.0), on cyber tasks, and in the small-model tier on every agentic benchmark. All benchmark numbers are vendor self-reported unless marked. Findings 14 to 17, 39 to 42.

## Options for the planner
Hard requirements applied: published evidence only; a license that does not block opening the repo later; nothing captured leaves the machine until the owner rules, so an option is ranked for the on-device job only if it fits a laptop, and hosted options are ranked only with a quoted retention term. Ranked by date of current form.

1. **Kimi K3.1 Pro** (2026-11-04)
   - What: 2.8T-A104B open-weight agentic model, 1M context, vision; hosted call only (Moonshot or third-party hosts).
   - Fit: fits the hosted-task-agent job; fails on-device; weights license is custom (read it before relying on redistribution); Moonshot's own terms allow service-improvement use of content unless an enterprise agreement says otherwise, so route it through a ZDR host (Fireworks default ZDR, OpenRouter ZDR routing, Bedrock mode none) rather than Moonshot's API if the owner allows any off-machine inference.
   - Tradeoffs: closest open-weight model to Opus 5.5 on computer use and terminal tasks per Moonshot's table; $2 and $10 pricing; self-reported numbers only.
   - Health: last commit (HF) within the week of 2026-11-04, latest release Kimi-K3.1-Pro, releases in last 12 months 5 (K2.6, K2.7-Code, K3, K3.1 Pro, plus K2.5), open discussions 15, archived no, license custom "kimi-k3.1-pro".
   - Evidence: findings 12, 14, 17.
2. **DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (deepseek-flash)** (2026-10-17)
   - What: 284B-A13B MIT-licensed multimodal model, 1M context; the cheapest hosted option at $0.30 and $1.20 peak.
   - Fit: hosted-task-agent job on cheap tier; fails on-device (284B); DeepSeek's API retention policy page was unreachable, so DeepSeek's own API is not rankable on retention; MIT weights can be served by a ZDR host instead.
   - Tradeoffs: cost is 30 to 40 times below frontier; Terminal-Bench 4.0 42.3 and OSWorld V2.1 69.3 trail K3.1 Pro and the closed models.
